## Qualitative feedback

### Case `2008_1629`

Display 1: All five citation IDs and reporter references are present inline, but they are visually indistinguishable from the passage text. A reader must parse dense paragraphs to identify which court said what. The five passages on pay-revision (Karnataka, A.P., Kerala, Armed Forces NPA) span different factual contexts; the flat format makes it difficult to assess each source's relevance to the core issue of post-resignation entitlement to retrospective pay revision. The disclaimer is present but buried at the bottom of an undifferentiated block.

Display 2: The authority table (E1–E5) immediately provides a navigable index with full citation coordinates. Each evidence block is labeled, making it trivial to jump to a specific passage. The separated 'Conclusion' and 'Uncertainty' sections are visually distinct. The structure helps a reader notice that sources span multiple pay-revision contexts (Karnataka teachers, A.P. pensioners, Armed Forces NPA) and evaluate relevance independently.

Comparison: The structured display is clearly superior for responsible legal research. The authority index acts as a table of contents, and the labeled evidence blocks allow a practitioner to quickly evaluate each source's relevance to the narrow issue (post-resignation entitlement to retrospective pay revision). The unstructured version buries the same information in a wall of text, increasing the risk that a researcher treats tangentially relevant passages (e.g., E4 on Armed Forces NPA) as directly on-point.
